A World of Conflicts

In 2025, 30% of the world countries had active armed conflicts across the globe.

From civil wars to insurgencies, millions of people live under the threat of violence every day.

We see that 9% of countries have recorded between 10 and 100 fatalities this year — already a worrying signal of widespread instability.

The situation becomes even more severe when we move up the scale: 11% of countries have suffered between 100 and 1,000 fatalities, placing them in the category of medium-intensity conflicts.

But the most alarming figure comes last: 10% of the world's countries have already endured more than 1,000 fatalities each this year — a stark reminder of how concentrated and devastating modern conflicts have become.

Let’s now look more closely at which countries are behind these numbers.

Looking more closely at the countries behind the highest number of fatalities, a clear pattern emerges: Ukraine, Sudan, Palestine, Myanmar, Nigeria, and Somalia are currently among the deadliest conflicts in the world in 2025.

The real question, however, is whether the world has been paying the proper level of attention to each of them over the past year — or if some tragedies remain far more visible than others.

Measuring the Gap

By comparing conflict fatalities to news articles published, we can quantify the attention gap.

Some conflicts see one article per 100 casualties. Others see one per 1,000. This disparity shapes public awareness and policy responses.
